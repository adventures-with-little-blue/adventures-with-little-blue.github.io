# Oil change and replacing the alternator cover gasket

Time to change the engine oil in Little Blue. At the same time, I decided to try to fix the small oil leak around the alternator cover. The grommet around the alternator cable is probably letting a little oil through. The leak is hardly noticeable, but it is enough to leave an irritating layer of black grime around the alternator cable. Since I have to drain the engine oil anyway, it seemed like an excellent opportunity to replace the gasket too.

<!--more-->

It had been 11,500 km since the last oil change, and with winter approaching, it was time to change the oil. The manual recommends changing it every 12,000 km, so it was due anyway. I went for a short ride to warm the oil up properly. I thought the road was completely dry, but apparently it was wet in places after all. When I got back, the freshly cleaned Blue had splashes of dirty water on it again. Sigh.

![Oil change](01-spillolja.jpg "Out with the old oil.")

Out with the old oil while the engine was still warm. Ugh. Looking at the foul-smelling mixture, I thought I might be better off changing the oil a little more often than the manual recommends. At least I couldn't see any metal or anything but oil in the mixture. Of course, I also changed the oil filter and fitted a new copper washer to the drain plug.

Next, it was time to replace the alternator cover gasket. I had bought the new gasket back when I [thought I would need to replace the stator](../2025-05-23-charger/). In theory, this should be a fairly quick job, but as usual, nothing ever goes particularly quickly for me.

I wasn't sure whether the bolts were different sizes, so I used this stylish bolt holder made from a cornflakes box to keep track of them. I traced the outline using the new gasket as a template.

![Bolt layout](02-bultordning.jpg "Keeping the bolts in order.")

Getting the cover off was no problem. I tapped it gently with a rubber mallet, and it came loose easily. Then the problems began. The gasket was firmly stuck to both the engine block and the generator cover. I tried wetting it with Brakleen and acetone, but nothing helped. The only plastic scraper I could find wore out quickly, while the gasket residue stayed firmly put.

![Cover removed](03-kapan_bort.jpg "The alternator cover is off. The gasket isn't.")

There was nothing for it but to drive to Motonet and pick up some gasket remover, "Gasket Remover". And a plastic scraper. Once again, a small and easy job had turned into something that was, first, not so small and easy, and second, a job that required new tools.

By the time I finally got started scraping, it was almost bedtime. Cleaning the cover itself went fairly well. I sprayed on the gasket remover and let it work for 20 minutes. Then the gasket came off with a bit of scrubbing. It still took two applications to remove all the residue.

![Cover cleaned](04-kapan_rengjord.jpg "The alternator cover and stator. The gasket has now been removed and the mating surface cleaned.")

The engine block was much worse. First, I had to work on one small section at a time because I needed to cover the areas where I didn't want gasket residue to end up. Second, some spots were really difficult to reach. So there I was, on my knees for half the night in the light of a rechargeable work lamp, sniffing solvent instead of getting my beauty sleep.

![Scraping off the gasket](05-skrapa_packning.jpg "So boring. Even with Gasket Remover, the gasket has to be scraped off one layer at a time.")

With all the scrapers worn out and the clock well past midnight, I finally got the last of the gasket residue off. Then I put the new gasket in place. I applied a little gasket sealant around the stator cable grommet and refitted the cover. Then I put the other covers back on as well. At last, it was time to top up with fresh engine oil. I added 3.1 litres this time (usually 3.05 l is enough, but since the alternator cover had been off, I figured I had managed to drain the engine more thoroughly this time).

Then it was time to call it a night.

The next morning, the garage still reeked of solvent and old oil. I usually do my day job from my home office in the garage, but to avoid the smell, I decided to go to the actual office instead. Since the road was dry and the forecast promised a sunny, rain-free day, I took Little Blue out for a test ride as well. No visible oil leaks so far, so the gasket replacement seems to have worked. Maybe I'm imagining it, but the gears seem to shift more easily with the new oil. What didn't go so well was that the promised dry weather turned into a proper downpour on the way home. Little Blue is filthy again.
