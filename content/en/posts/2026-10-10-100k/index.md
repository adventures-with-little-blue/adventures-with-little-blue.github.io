# I set off on an old wreck and came home with a barely run-in gem?

My, how time flies, or at least the miles do. In the spring, [Little Blue reached 90,000 km](../2026-05-13-levaneva-och-90k/#90000-km). Now, somewhat unplanned, it was time for the next milestone – 100,000 km. But there was one problem: the odometer only goes up to 99,999.9 km, and we were really close to that. What happens when the odometer has to continue past 99,999.9? My daughter guessed that the bike would suddenly and spectacularly fall into a thousand pieces, like something from Donald Duck. I thought that all of Blue's ailments would cure themselves and that I could ride home on an almost unworn old gem.

<!--more-->

The day brought temperatures of around +5 °C and strong, gusty winds, but the sun came out now and then. I had planned to spend the day at home doing various chores. I was actually just about to give Little Blue a foam bath when I was ordered to deliver some forgotten papers to my daughter, who was in town on a coaching assignment. So I just had to quickly wipe the foam off the bike and wrap up warm for a short ride. This time I actually had a proper reason to take the bike, because one car was away and I had just connected the battery charger to the other one. To avoid disrupting CTEK's algorithms, it is best not to disconnect it again before it is finished.

![Foam bath](01-skumbad.jpg "Little Blue gets a foam bath.")

A longer ride in these temperatures requires plenty of clothing, but I was only heading into town briefly, so I settled for putting the winter lining into my riding trousers and set off. Only when I was sitting on the bike did I realise how close we were to the 100k mark. If I extended the day's ride a little, we could celebrate the occasion on a reasonably nice day, instead of merely noting that the milestone had passed on the way to work. So, after I had delivered the forgotten papers, we headed for the Replot Bridge. The wind gauge showed 16 m/s on the bridge, and the wind pulled and tugged hard from the side as we crossed it. I then quite shamelessly left the future celebrant alone in the windy car park while I went into [Berny's](https://www.berny.fi/en) to order pizza.

![Berny's](02-bernys.jpg "Waiting for pizza.")

![Replot Bridge](03-replotbron.jpg "Little Blue at the Replot Bridge.")

Strong winds and low temperatures cooled the engine down quickly. When I tried to start it again after a 45-minute break, I had to use full choke, as though the engine had been completely cold. But then we puttered off again, across the bridge and through Jungsund, Singsby, Karperö and Smedsby towards Old Vaasa. It was on the cold side; I certainly missed the long underwear and fleece I would have put on if I had known we were going to ride more than 30 km.

The big moment was now drawing near. I hoped to make it to the tree lined avenue by Korsholm Church, but it was too far away. The odometer showed 99,999.9 just before the railway crossing in Runsor. I rode up onto the footpath and then puttered across the railway crossing with my phone in my hand to record the whole thing. Had my daughter's Donald Duck scenario come true, the bike would have flown into a thousand pieces right on the tracks. It would in fact have been a trick worthy of [Wile E. Coyote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wile_E._Coyote_and_the_Road_Runner) himself to then be run over by the train as well :smile:.

![99,999.9 km](04-gammal.jpg "99,999.9 km.")

We made it across the tracks intact in any case. As far as I know, not a single nut fell off when the odometer rolled over to zero. That is what happened, after all. All that remained was to test my theory that all of Blue's ailments would heal themselves. To my disappointment, I could establish that the steering still has a notch, the chain still rattles, and the engine still does not run completely smoothly below 4,000 rpm. So the last few kilometres home were ridden on the same old wreck as before. Thankfully, I have ordered most of the parts; time to start wrenching.
