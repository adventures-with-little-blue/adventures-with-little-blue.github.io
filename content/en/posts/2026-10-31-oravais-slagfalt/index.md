# An autumn ride to the Oravais battlefield

The riding season must really be drawing to a close. The days are short, and the roads never quite dry out. After a two-week break, I was starting to miss riding. So today I took the opportunity to go for a ride, fully aware of the challenges that lay ahead.

<!--more-->

I had a mishap a couple of weeks ago while drilling a piece of metal. The workpiece hit my left thumb pretty hard, taking off the nail, and the wound had to be stitched up. I had a large bandage wrapped around my thumb, so I couldn't get my riding gloves and the like on. On Friday, most of the dressing was removed, leaving just a slightly thicker-than-usual plaster. I managed to squeeze into my largest riding glove, although it wasn't very comfortable. So this was the first time in two weeks that I was able to ride a motorcycle at all.

The forecast promised +5°, partly cloudy skies and no rain. Still, I think the thermometer stayed just below 5 all day, and the partly cloudy skies amounted to three minutes of pale sunshine when I could see my shadow. The rest of the day was heavily overcast. And that promise of no rain turned out to include quite a bit of drizzle, actually.

So I had to prepare for cold and damp conditions. I was also well aware that at this time of year the roads never dry out; instead, they get a permanent coating of water mixed with mud from farm machinery. Those were the conditions, and I was ready. I fitted Little Blue with extra fabric hand covers around the grips (Hippo Hands style, but a cheap copy). Unfortunately, they're a little too small to fit over the ugly plastic hand guards I always have on, but I managed to secure them anyway. The downside is that I can't reach the turn-signal switch, at least not with my injured left thumb. Instead, I had to pull my hand out of the cover every time I wanted to use the turn signal. Naturally, my heated grips were also roasting away under the hand covers; setting 2/5 was plenty.

![Hand covers](handskydd.jpg "Hand covers – like big, warm mittens.")


I dressed in a thicker balaclava under my helmet, an extra neck warmer, six layers on my upper body and five on my lower body. My outermost layer was my rain suit, which protected me from dampness and cold wind as well as keeping my riding gear clean from all the dirty water flying around. Ski socks and knitted socks inside my riding boots completed the outfit.

All in all, the ride went well. After a couple of hours my feet got a little cold, but otherwise it wasn't too cold. My injured thumb hurt a little, but it seems to have made it through without the wound starting to bleed again, so I'll count that as a win. The biggest problem is really all the gear that has to be put on and taken off – you don't feel like stopping for coffee when you know you have to take off your dirty rain gear and then put it back on again. And I had my phone in the pocket of my riding trousers, so it wasn't very easy to fish out from under my rain trousers. I probably missed a few nice photos because of that.

The route mostly followed familiar roads. Via Toby to Kolkki, then north along the Kyrö River to Isokyrö. From there to Komossa, then along a slippery, soft gravel road to Alahärmä, followed by Voltti and Jeppo. One road I'd never ridden before crossed the bridge over the Lapua River at Lassila and then followed Kyrkvägen back into Jeppo.

From Jeppo I continued through Pensala to Oravais. I took a photo of the church and then rode out to Furirbostället by the battlefield.

![Oravais Church](oravais_kyrka.jpg "Oravais Church.")

![Furirbostället](furirbostallet.jpg "Furirbostället by the Oravais battlefield.")

The [Battle of Oravais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Oravais) on 14 September 1808 was the Swedish army's last serious attempt to stop the advancing Russian forces during the [Finnish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_War). More Swedish troops fell there than in any other battle, and it can be considered the battle that finally decided the war. After this, the Russians continued north along the Ostrobothnian coast and then into Sweden, as far as Skellefteå. They also marched across the ice and took Umeå. The territories they occupied in what is now Sweden were later handed back, but Finland became part of Russia. Worth keeping in mind. For us Finns, the threat from the east feels more relevant again than it has in a long time.

![The Oravais battlefield](oravais_slagfalt.jpg "The Oravais battlefield.")

Today is [All Saints' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints%27_Day), a day to remember the departed. Tradition has it that people light candles at the graves of deceased relatives and friends. On my way home from Oravais, I stopped at the Vörå cemetery, where some of my late relatives are buried.

![All Saints' Day](allhelgona.jpg "On All Saints' Day, we remember the generations who came before us.")


I rode 216 km on the last day of October, with an outside temperature of +4.5°C. The ride went more or less as expected: cold, wet and very muddy. My thumb ached a little, but really, things went better than expected. I had other plans for the afternoon; otherwise, I might have gone for a slightly longer ride, perhaps with a short walk halfway through to warm up a little. But it will probably be hard to find time for more rides this year, even if the weather stays above freezing. We'll see.
