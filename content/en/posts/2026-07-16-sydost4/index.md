# Around Mikkeli and then home

_The final day of our four-day trip in southeastern Finland._

Today it was time to start heading home, but not by the shortest route. The plan was first to visit all the remaining municipalities in South Savo and perhaps have time for something exciting on the way home as well. The expected highlight of the day was probably Puumala. On the way home, there might be a visit to Petäjävesi Old Church if time allowed.

<!--more-->

The planned route for the day was long, over 800 km. So once again, it was important to get going early. At 6:55, the freeze-dried breakfast had been eaten, morning coffee brewed, everything packed and the overnight spot tidied up. Time to leave. This morning the start actually felt a little sluggish. It was a little chilly and my body felt somewhat stiff. Three fairly intense and long days were beginning to take their toll. But quite soon it began to feel fun again. The road towards Mäntyharju may not be very exciting, but it is in good condition.

Right on the border between Kouvola (formerly Valkeala) and Mäntyharju, which is also the border between the regions of Kymenlaakso and South Savo, lies the Voikoski rapids, where gas supplier Woikoski Oy has its factory and head office. There is a factory museum that would have been interesting to visit, but it was of course not open at that hour. With many kilometres to ride, I simply passed by.

## Around Mikkeli

We were now back in South Savo. The first stop was Mäntyharju. In Mäntyharju, I photographed the church, then refuelled and filled up my drinking water at S-Market. The town left no deep impression. I continued in the direction of Ristiina.

![Mäntyharju Church](mantyharjus_kyrka.jpg "Mäntyharju Church.")

There was not much life in Ristiina at eight in the morning. Ristiina was formerly a municipality of its own but was incorporated into the city of Mikkeli in 2013. Here too, I photographed the church and moved on.

![Ristiina Church](kristina_kyrka.jpg "Ristiina Church.")

In retrospect, I concluded that I made a poor route choice here. There were two roads to choose from: gravel road 4321 heading northeast towards Anttola, and the slightly longer paved road 4323 that first went southeast and then turned north towards Puumala. Not to miss Anttola, I chose the gravel road. It was winding, hilly and freshly graded, with lots of loose gravel, and rather difficult to ride. In addition, I had to travel behind a dusty truck for a fair distance. And there was not particularly much to see. To top it all off, Anttola’s centre was not much to see either; it is probably the beaches that draw people there. The other road would likely have offered more relaxed riding and prettier views, probably worth missing Anttola for.

Anttola, then. Formerly a municipality in its own right, it was incorporated into Mikkeli in 2001. The former municipality should not be confused with the village of Anttola in Savonlinna. I took a couple of photos in the centre and then continued towards Puumala.

![Anttola Church](anttolas_kyrka.jpg "Anttola Church.")

![Anttola centre](anttola_centrum.jpg "Anttola centre.")

Along the road towards Puumala, you cross Lake Lietvesi. There is a rest area with a viewpoint there. I stopped to sip some coffee and enjoy the fantastically beautiful view. This is an absolutely wonderful place; the photos do not do it justice. I sat there enjoying the view and the morning sun, which was now beginning to warm things more and more. After a while, I was joined by a talkative gentleman who said that he was a grave digger for Mikkeli’s parish union and was on his way to Puumala on business. He was also a motorcyclist. We talked for quite a while about this and that.

![Lietvesi rest area](lietvesi1.jpg "Lietvesi rest area.")

![Lietvesi observation tower](lietvesi2.jpg "Lietvesi observation tower.")

Then on to Puumala. There was already activity down by the harbour. Unfortunately, I did not have time for a longer break; otherwise it would have been nice to have a coffee and look at the bridge. [The Puumalansalmi Bridge](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puumalansalmen_silta) (fi), right beside Puumala harbour, is truly impressive to look at.

![Puumala Church](puumalas_kyrka.jpg "Puumala Church.")

![Puumala](puumala.jpg "Puumala.")

Then I made the day’s second poor route choice. On the way to Sulkava, we crossed the bridge and then turned north. Here again there were two options: the Kietävälä ferry, running straight north towards Sulkava, or a longer detour east and then the Rongonsalmi ferry to road 438 towards Sulkava. I chose the first option. The road was in poor condition and rather boring. I do not know exactly what I passed up, but at least the Telataipale Canal and the fairly recently built Veransalmi Bridge. Well, hindsight is easy.

At least I again had perfect timing with the Kietävälä ferry. It arrived in port just as I did. There was hardly a rush, of course. I was alone on the ferry, with the decidedly unexciting name _Lautta 150_. Incidentally, the road ferry _Nestori_ operated the Kietävälä route from 2012 to 2020. In 2020, Nestori was moved to Arvinsalmi, [where we rode on it](../2026-07-14-sydost2/) two days ago.

We then followed the rather boring and rather poor road north to Sulkava. I photographed the church and we moved on. Sulkava left no strong impression.

## Mikkeli

To save some time, we now took the motorway into Mikkeli, almost 40 km. Boring but efficient. Mikkeli has several interesting war-themed museums, including the Headquarters Museum, where commander-in-chief [Mannerheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gustaf_Emil_Mannerheim) had his office. I have visited the museum before, and today the timetable did not allow a return visit. Mikkeli also has an important strategic role today. NATO’s new Multinational Corps Land Component Command for Northern Europe has been located in Mikkeli and began operating this year.

In Mikkeli, I had planned to have lunch. But somehow I missed the planned lunch place. The centre was a little confusing with various street works and closed streets. I photographed [the cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikkeli_Cathedral) and then the parish church, which had been the church of Mikkeli Rural Municipality. The rural municipality was merged with the city in 2001, at the same time as Anttola.

![Mikkeli Cathedral](stmichels_domkyrka.jpg "Mikkeli Cathedral.")

![The parish church](sockenkyrkan.jpg "The parish church.")

Before I knew it, I had ridden out of the centre without lunch. I refuelled at ABC but did not feel like eating their lunch, so we continued towards Hirvensalmi. On the way there, we made a short tour of the village of Otava, one of the former rural municipality’s largest villages. I photographed Otava village church, now perhaps demoted from a church to a parish hall.

![Otava village church](otavas_kyrka.jpg "Otava village church.")

## Hirvensalmi and Kangasniemi

Hirvensalmi is South Savo’s second-smallest municipality by population, after Enonkoski. The municipal centre seemed cosy. So cosy, in fact, that I decided to have lunch at the village’s largest lunch restaurant, _Puulanaitta_. Really good food, and it was nice to sit in the sun for a while. After the obligatory photo of the church, we continued towards Kangasniemi.

![Hirvensalmi Church](hirvensalmis_kyrka.jpg "Hirvensalmi Church.")

![Hirvensalmi Puulanaitta](hirvensalmi_puulanaitta.jpg "Hirvensalmi Puulanaitta.")

The distance between Hirvensalmi and Kangasniemi is not particularly long. The only problem is that the large lake [Puula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puula) is in the way, so unless you travel by boat, it becomes quite a substantial detour. The detour actually led right into the outskirts of Joutsa municipality in Central Finland, but we will return to that municipality later today. So it was almost 70 km along mostly rather straight and boring roads before we reached Kangasniemi. Just before Kangasniemi, I actually got to ride behind three other motorcyclists, among the fairly few I had seen on this trip. They soon turned off in another direction, in any case. We did not have time to explore Kangasniemi any more closely; I took a photo of the church and then we continued towards Toivakka.

![Kangasniemi Church](kangasniemis_kyrka.jpg "Kangasniemi Church.")

With Kangasniemi, we had in fact visited every municipality in South Savo, all during this trip. But it was now time to change region from South Savo to Central Finland.

## Toivakka, Leivonmäki and Luhanka

As far as I know, Toivakka is chiefly known for two things: the defence forces’ large weapons depot, [Toivakan asevarikko](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toivakan_asevarikko) (fi), and the church’s ceiling paintings.

The church was renovated in the 1920s under the direction of [Alvar Aalto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvar_Aalto). Aalto made sketches for subdued ceiling paintings, which were executed by Wilho Wahlroos. By the 1970s, the ceiling paintings were in poor condition. Artist Pellervo Lukumies was hired to repaint the ceiling, surely with the intention that the original paintings would be restored. But the artist simply painted over Aalto’s already world-famous motifs and replaced them with colourful, almost psychedelic paintings of his own design. When the vicar saw the first completed paintings, he was probably seized by holy wrath. He took the keys from the artist and is said to have declared, “I do not preach in a circus!” The artist nevertheless got in through a roof window and continued his work; the paintings were completed in 1973. The vicar resigned. The ceiling paintings are now Toivakka’s foremost attraction.

![Toivakka Church](toivakkas_kyrka1.jpg "Toivakka Church.")

![Toivakka Church ceiling paintings](toivakkas_kyrka2.jpg "Toivakka Church ceiling paintings.")

It was now almost 14:30, and I had a number of places to ride through before the day’s next “big” attraction, Petäjävesi Church. With closing times to keep, there was no time to lose, so I swung back into the saddle and we headed south. I photographed Leivonmäki Church. Leivonmäki municipality merged with Joutsa in 2008. In hindsight, we would probably have had time to ride through Joutsa centre as well, but instead we continued towards Luhanka. So we can tick off Joutsa as visited, even though we did not go to the centre; we will have to return another time.

![Leivonmäki Church](leivonmakis_kyrka.jpg "Leivonmäki Church.")

Just northwest of Leivonmäki lies [Leivonmäki National Park](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/leivonmaki-national-park). The road towards Luhanka actually passes straight through the national park, but to be honest, I did not see anything exciting, just overgrown young forest, really. But perhaps this calls for a proper visit another time; camping in a tent is possible here.

Next, it was time to visit mainland Finland’s second-smallest municipality by population (2025: 695 inhabitants), Luhanka. Of mainland municipalities, only Lestijärvi (2025: 656 inhabitants) in North Ostrobothnia has fewer residents. The archipelago municipalities of Åland are in a class of their own, however, with Sottunga (2025: 101 inhabitants) as the extreme case. In any event, Luhanka’s little church village was truly cosy and apparently a popular summer destination. Plenty of people were sitting at outdoor cafés and strolling around. Apparently, there are a great many summer cottages in the municipality.

![Luhanka Church](luhangos_kyrka.jpg "Luhanka Church.")

## Korpilahti, Muurame and Säynätsalo

I must say that road 612 south along the shore of Lake Päijänne through Sysmä, across Pulkkilanharju and down to Vääksy in Asikkala looks very tempting, at least on the map. This trip nevertheless continued homewards, northwest. We soon entered the former municipality of Korpilahti, part of the city of Jyväskylä since 2009. Korpilahti is home to Oravivuori hill, with one of the measurement points of the [Struve Geodetic Arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struve_Geodetic_Arc), a UNESCO World Heritage Site. This is definitely a destination for a future trip. But Korpilahti also has another attraction up its sleeve, which was a surprise to me. [Kärkistensalmi Bridge](https://fi.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4rkisten_silta) (fi), across [Lake Päijänne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A4ij%C3%A4nne), was completed in 1997 and is apparently the country’s fourth-longest bridge.

![Kärkistensalmi Bridge](karkistensalmi_bro.jpg "Kärkistensalmi Bridge.")

Once we had crossed the bridge, we first turned southwest to Korpilahti church village. Then we turned back towards Muurame.

![Korpilahti Church](korpilax_kyrka.jpg "Korpilahti Church.")

Muurame municipality has somehow managed to remain independent despite being almost completely surrounded by Jyväskylä. The municipality also has a short water boundary with Toivakka out on Lake Päijänne. Apparently, they are doing well.

![Muurame Church](muurames_kyrka.jpg "Muurame Church.")

Outside Muurame centre lies the island of Säynätsalo, also a former municipality that merged with Jyväskylä in 1993. The former Säynätsalo municipality has two buildings included in [Aalto Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalto_Works), a property on [UNESCO’s World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Finland). [Säynätsalo Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A4yn%C3%A4tsalo_Town_Hall) in the centre, and Aalto’s Experimental House on the island of Muuratsalo. You can neither visit nor see the Experimental House from outside without a guided tour; nothing was visible from the road.

![Säynätsalo Town Hall](saynatsalos_stadshus.jpg "Säynätsalo Town Hall.")

## Petäjävesi

In any case, it was high time to set off for Petäjävesi so as not to miss the final guided tour of the old church. First road 9 towards Jyväskylä, then along road 18 westward. Originally, I had intended to make a detour through Jyväskylä centre, but that did not happen on this trip either.

[Petäjävesi Old Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet%C3%A4j%C3%A4vesi_Old_Church) lies right beside the main road, but to reach the car park, it is better to go via the centre first. This way, you pass both the new church and get to ride over the fine old bridge across Lake Petäjävesi.

![Petäjävesi New Church](petajavesi_nya_kyrka.jpg "Petäjävesi New Church.")

We made it in time for the day’s penultimate guided tour. The old church is included on the [World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Finland). The church was built in the 1760s by local peasants. Petäjävesi was at that time a sparsely populated area, far from larger settlements. The builders did not wait for permission or plans from the authorities, but built according to their own methods. The interior is simple and consists of untreated logs, with a rather unique way of locking each layer of logs together. Yet despite the craftsmanship, you can see that the work was done quickly and efficiently. In places, daylight can be seen through the log walls.

The church was impractical in that it was cold in winter and well into early summer. It also soon became too small. In 1879, the new church was completed, and the old one was then left entirely to its fate. For this reason, the old church retains its original appearance, which in most other churches was gradually modernised away. This is also why this particular church was added to the World Heritage List.

![Petäjävesi Old Church](petajavesi_gamla_kyrka1.jpg "Petäjävesi Old Church.")

![Interior](petajavesi_gamla_kyrka2.jpg "Interior.")

![Old burial ground](petajavesi_gamla_kyrka3.jpg "Old burial ground.")

## Home via Multia, Ähtäri and Töysä

We took the opportunity to refuel in Petäjävesi and then continued northwest through the vast forests to Multia and then onward to Ähtäri. Multia is one of the places we had previously only ridden through on the outskirts, but now we rode through the centre and looked at the church. The church appears newly painted and looked somewhat like a manor house, with all its decorative woodwork and embellishments.

![Multia Church](muldias_kyrka.jpg "Multia Church.")

In Ähtäri, I stopped only to photograph the church. Beside it is also an old church boat, the kind residents of outlying villages used to row to the church village for compulsory worship.

![Ähtäri Church](etseris_kyrka.jpg "Ähtäri Church.")

![Church boat, Ähtäri](kyrkbat_etseri.jpg "Church boat, Ähtäri.")

Further west, in Töysä, I turned off and photographed the church. Then we continued north, first along road 7060 and then road 7072 towards Kuortane. The latter in particular, I simply do not understand. Excellent asphalt; a winding, pleasant road running through almost nothing but forest. Only a few homes along the entire route. I think one tractor came towards me; otherwise, I did not see a living soul. You wonder who such roads are kept in top condition for. The sun shone a little into my eyes; otherwise, all that remained was to enjoy it.

![Töysä Church](toysas_kyrka.jpg "Töysä Church.")

But then only the boring transport stretch remained. Through Seinäjoki and all the way home. It was 21:40 by the time we got home, so perhaps one would not have wanted to ride much later that day.

![Home again](hemma_igen.jpg "Home again.")

## Route

It was quite a long day to finish a fairly intense trip. The odometer showed 837 km. There were quite a few rather boring road sections today, with straight roads through large forests. But we also had time for some highlights, such as the Lietvesi rest area, Puumala and Petäjävesi Old Church. The best riding of the day was actually not in Savo but, surprisingly, the stretch of road between Töysä and Kuortane.
