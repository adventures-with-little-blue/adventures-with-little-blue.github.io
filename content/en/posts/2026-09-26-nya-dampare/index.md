# New rear shock absorbers

I invested in new rear shock absorbers. Perhaps as a [30,000 km together](../2026-09-20-30k-tillsammans/) present? I did not really know what to expect, but ... WHY did I wait 30,000 km?

<!--more-->

It was really a bit of an impulse purchase. I was ordering a bunch of other spare parts from Wemoto when I spotted shock absorbers made for the CB 500. They seemed rather affordable in the online shop (before VAT, customs duty, and shipping). In the end, however, they still cost almost €320, making them the most expensive single purchase for this bike so far. I went for a pair of YSS Plus Line E-series shocks, which are apparently YSS’s second most affordable option.

To remove the old shocks, I naturally had to take off the rear fairing but also the silencer. I had not removed it from the exhaust manifold before, but it was not a big job and went smoothly. Once both shocks were out, I took the opportunity to check the swingarm bearings. Smooth movement, no play – the bearings seem to be okay. They had otherwise been a suspected cause of the vibrations I have felt lately.

![Without shocks](utan_dampare.jpg "Without shock absorbers. Little Blue would probably look good with a mono-shock...")

Fitting the new shocks was not difficult either: first press them onto the upper mounting points, then fasten them at the bottom. Then it was time to put the silencer and fairings back on. I also adjusted and lubricated the chain, and then the job was done.

![New shocks fitted](monterade.jpg "New shock absorbers fitted.")

What can already be established is that adjusting the preload will be awkward. The old ones were difficult in the sense that I did not have a suitable tool, but it worked to push the spring back with a large screwdriver and then turn it by hand. Tools are needed to adjust the new shocks. First, one has to undo a small hex-socket locking screw, which is difficult to reach under the fairings and will also be full of mud and dust before long. Then one has to turn the silver-coloured adjuster with the supplied tool (a suitable screwdriver would probably work too). But the adjuster is also difficult to reach behind the fairings. Furthermore, the adjustment is stepless, so one has to take care to adjust both sides equally. I will probably have to remove the rear fairing to adjust the suspension, which in practice means that it will rarely, if ever, be adjusted. A step backwards.

![Adjustment](justering.jpg "Adjustment is awkward.")

I did not take a test ride until the following day, and ... well, what a difference! I had thought that shock absorbers were probably something only riders with experience of many different motorcycles would notice. But I too noticed how much more stable and even it felt.

The old shocks had been set with too much preload, since they tended to bottom out anyway. As soon as I sat on the bike with the new shocks, it felt as though there was more sag, as there should be. I suppose I will have to try to set it to the right level at some point. These should at least not bottom out as easily.

When we set off, there was a truly noticeable difference in how calmly the rear end behaved. I am used to the rear end bouncing around a little at every small unevenness, but now the small irregularities disappeared entirely. Gravel and small bumps felt considerably less pronounced than before. Larger bumps can still be felt, of course, but the bike seems to settle a little faster after a bump than before. I started to feel that the front fork was slow and clumsy instead, which I had never noticed before. In any case, the whole bike felt calmer on the road somehow. It showed on tarmac in that I found myself at a much higher speed than I had intended – I usually feel when it is time to stop accelerating, but that feeling came later now. The biggest difference was perhaps on gravel roads, though. Where I had previously felt that 70 km/h was appropriate, 90 km/h now felt completely comfortable. The bike also felt much more stable and confidence-inspiring on loose gravel.

It remains to be tested how the new shocks work with luggage or a passenger. Perhaps the preload needs adjusting then. But I cannot see why the new ones would not be considerably better than the old ones in those situations too. After two short test rides, I can therefore say that they feel good, and one thought inevitably occurs: WHY have I ridden 30,000 km with the old shocks?

One problem the new shocks did not solve was the vibrations I feel. The smoother shocks do reduce some vibrations from the tyres and swingarm, of course, but the shocks were clearly not the cause of the problem. I also checked the swingarm bearings, which seemed to be in good condition. So the question remains: Where are the vibrations coming from? The tyres were changed this year. The steering-head bearing is clearly worn and needs replacing when I have time, but it does not feel as though it is causing the vibrations, at least not all of them. The rear-wheel bearings feel okay. The front-wheel bearings felt okay when I last checked them, but I will soon lift the front end and check again. The engine still runs reasonably smoothly, so I do not think uneven running is the culprit. What remains to check? Main bearings or crankshaft bearings? That sounds like a nightmare scenario, but the symptoms do not seem right for that either. Well, it will presumably become clearer over time.
