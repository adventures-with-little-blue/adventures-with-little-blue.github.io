# Hiking with friends

A long-planned hike with friends in Helvetinjärvi National Park finally happened. Even though it was already October, the weather was good enough that I decided to ride my motorcycle to the hike. We met on Friday afternoon, but unfortunately my friends had other plans from Saturday evening onwards. I decided to continue on to Liesjärvi National Park for a second night out. It also gave me the opportunity to visit a few new municipalities.

<!--more-->

Getting to a hiking destination by motorcycle creates some logistical challenges. A hiking backpack is large and awkward to pack on the bike. You also need both hiking gear and riding gear – very few items work for both. In any case, we had not planned a particularly demanding hike; the campsites were only a little over a kilometre from the car park. Carrying the panniers would have been possible, but quite a struggle. For the challenge, I thought I would try to bring all my hiking gear. In the end, it worked out quite well. My fully packed hiking backpack fitted inside my large waterproof dry bag, as long as I removed the tent.

![Packed backpack](01-rinkka.jpg "Packed 90 L backpack.")

The tent travelled in one of the panniers instead. The extra riding gear, such as a fleece, rain gear and spare gloves, also fitted in the panniers, along with a tarpaulin for Little Blue. The only compromise I had to make was to leave my large hiking boots at home and take a pair of low trail-running shoes instead. The shoes worked well on this trip, but they could be a problem in rougher terrain. For day trips, the tank bag works well, just as it has on my other trips.

## On the way to Helvetinjärvi National Park

Almost everything was packed by Thursday evening. I worked a shorter day on Friday, packed the chilled food into the panniers and set off.

![Ready to set off](02-redo.jpg "Ready to set off.")

The weather forecast had promised a dry day with a little sunshine – in practice, there were thick clouds and occasional drizzle. It was warm, though, so riding was no problem. I filled up in Kurikka and then stopped to photograph a few churches along the way: Kurikka, Jalasjärvi and Virrat.

![Kurikka church](03-kurikka_kyrka.jpg "Kurikka church.")

![Jalasjärvi church](04-jalasjarvi_kyrka.jpg "Jalasjärvi church.")

![Virrat church](05-virdois_kyrka.jpg "Virrat church.")

South of Virrat are the Toriseva gorge lakes. I took a short break at the café hut. It was closed for the season, but I admired the view and actually found a bunch of chanterelles, which I picked.

![Toriseva café hut](06-toriseva1.jpg "Toriseva café hut.")

![Alainen-Toriseva](07-toriseva2.jpg "Lake Alainen-Toriseva.")

I also rode into Visuvesi, mostly because of its funny name. Down by the water, in the middle of an industrial area, are the village swimming beach and a small chapel, Kulkurin Kappeli.

![Kulkurin Chapel](09-kulkurin_kappeli.jpg "Kulkurin Kappeli in Visuvesi.")

I continued via Ruovesi and picked up a few supplies before setting off along the long gravel roads towards the Haukanhieta campsite in [Helvetinjärvi National Park](https://www.luontoon.fi/en/destinations/helvetinjarvi-national-park). The sun actually came out in Ruovesi, but as soon as we turned northwest, it disappeared again behind thick clouds.

![Ruovesi church](10-ruovesi_kyrka.jpg "Ruovesi church.")


## Helvetinjärvi National Park

I met my friends at the car park. We pitched our tents on the beach, cooked some food and sat chatting. On Saturday we went on a day hike to Helvetinkolu and back. The weather was grey and miserable, with drizzle most of the time, but the scenery was beautiful and our spirits were high.

![Helvetinjärvi National Park](12-helvetinjarvi1.jpg "Helvetinjärvi National Park.")

![Helvetinkolu](14-helvetinjarvi3.jpg "Below Helvetinkolu. A bit of 'been there, done that'.")

Unfortunately, my friends had other plans for Saturday evening, so when we returned from our hike to Helvetinkolu, we said goodbye and each went our separate ways.


## On the way to Liesjärvi

I had already planned to carry on, but in fact I had two options. The shorter option would have been Kauhaneva-Pohjankangas National Park, on the border between Kauhajoki and Isojoki. That would have meant a short ride on Saturday, a suitable trail to walk on Sunday and a short ride home. The other option was considerably longer: to Liesjärvi National Park in Tammela. Since it was already afternoon, there was no way I could reach Liesjärvi before dark. The weather was still unsettled, with drizzle and thick clouds, and Sunday’s forecast was even wetter. So the first option should have been an obvious choice, but somehow I ended up heading south towards Tammela.

Since it was clear that there would not be enough daylight, I shortened the route a little. The most important destination for the day was Karkkila, which I had not visited with Little Blue before. Still, I could not bring myself to take the fastest route, via the Tampere motorway down to Riihimäki and then through Loppi to Karkkila. Instead, I chose a much more enjoyable, more easterly route along roads I had not ridden before.

The route went via Orivesi, Eräjärvi (a former municipality, part of Orivesi since 1973), Kuhmalahti (a former municipality, part of Kangasala since 2011), Luopioinen (a former municipality, part of Pälkäne since 2007) and Hauho (a former municipality, part of Hämeenlinna since 2009). Some of the roads were really enjoyable on a motorcycle, but at the same time I kept worrying that the daylight hours were running out. It drizzled almost all the time, and the clouds were so thick that it felt like dusk even though the sun was still up. There is also something wrong with the drivetrain, probably a stiff link in the chain. It rattles horribly, so the ride was not exactly a great pleasure.

In the village of Renko, I nearly collided with a deer that was apparently eating someone’s berry bushes. I was not going very fast, but it certainly startled me.

![Renko church](20-renko_kyrka.jpg "Renko church.")

The ride to Piplala actually went well. The last stretch down to Karkkila was properly dark. I took a short ride through the town. I skipped the old industrial area and only photographed the church before continuing on. I have been to Karkkila before, but not with Little Blue.

![Karkkila church](23-hogfors_kyrka.jpg "Karkkila church.")

From Karkkila to the turn-off for the national park, you ride along the wide Pori road. Then it was quite a shock to turn onto the little, bumpy gravel road for the last couple of kilometres. A dirty headlight, a heavily loaded motorcycle and a bumpy road with loose gravel in pitch darkness are not exactly a comfortable combination. I was starting to wonder whether this had really been such a good idea.

## Liesjärvi National Park

![It gets dark](25-morkt.jpg "It gets dark.")

At the car park, I rearranged my luggage and covered Little Blue with the tarpaulin I had brought along. It was pitch-dark, so I was completely dependent on my torch. I left my helmet and boots in the panniers under the tarpaulin, hoping they would not grow legs. Then I set off for the little over one-kilometre walk along slippery boardwalks through a dense, completely dark forest. The campsite was almost full; there must have been thirty tents or more. I walked around the area twice before finding a place to pitch my tent. It was reasonably flat, but far too central for me to expect an undisturbed night’s sleep. Then it was time for the day’s lunch, dinner and supper – grilled sausages and a sip of red wine.

![Sausages for dinner](26-korv.jpg "Sausages for dinner.")

The next day began with fine weather. I had some breakfast while packing up the tent. When I left the camping area at 8:45, I was among the very first to go. I packed most of my things onto Little Blue and then went for a walk across the national park’s main attraction, the narrow esker Kyynäränharju between two lakes.

![Liesjärvi National Park](28-liesjarvi1.jpg "Liesjärvi National Park.")

![Liesjärvi National Park](29-liesjarvi2.jpg "Liesjärvi National Park.")

It was time to head home. I removed the tarpaulin, made the final adjustments to my luggage and set off. The weather was still decent, but rain was forecast for noon.

## The way home

I just had to ride through Sommarnäs (Somerniemi, a former municipality that has been part of Somero since 1977), just because of the name. The place was named after a nobleman who owned the local Palikais estate in the 17th century.

![Sommarnäs church](31-sommarnas_kyrka.jpg "Sommarnäs church.")

As we rode towards Somero, the sky grew darker and darker. We photographed the church and then took the “back road” towards Koski on the south side of Lake Kirkkojärvi. We continued along gravel roads for a short while and came to a bridge that looked decidedly suspicious. The frame was wooden, but apparently the deck was no longer in very good condition, as thick metal plates had been laid over most of the roadway. Even so, there were holes large enough for a motorcycle tyre to fall through. It was probably 3–4 metres down to the flowing water. Well, all I could do was hope for the best and ride on.

![Somero church](32-somero_kyrka.jpg "Somero church.")

Just after the bridge, the first raindrops began to fall. Although the rain soon stopped again, I decided it was better to be safe than sorry and put on my rain gear and winter gloves.

![The rain begins](33-regnet_borjar.jpg "The rain begins.")

The rain let up for a short while, but when we reached Koski Tl, it was absolutely pouring. “Tl” in the municipality’s Finnish name stands for Turun lääni (the Province of Turku), distinguishing it from Koski Hl, or Hämeen lääni (the Province of Tavastia). The latter place is now called Hämeenkoski (now part of Hollola), but Koski Tl has kept its suffix.

Koski seemed like a lovely place. The bell tower stood at one end of the village and the beautiful church at the other. The Paimionjoki river flows through the village and forms rapids in the church village, which have been used for hydropower. A museum and recreation area remain. It would have been well worth a walk, if not for the weather.

![Koski church](34-koskis_kyrka.jpg "Koski church.")

The road from Somero to Koski was enjoyable to ride, and it only got better the farther south we went – winding roads with good surfaces and varied scenery. The road is called [Hämeen Härkätie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%A4meen_H%C3%A4rk%C3%A4tie) and has been in use since at least the 13th century. Despite the rain, riding it was a real pleasure. It was not wet enough for large puddles to form, so there were no major problems with tyre grip.

Soon we were in Marttila. In many ways it resembled Koski: a small and rather cosy place.

![Marttila church](35-st_martens_kyrka.jpg "Marttila church.")

We followed the rolling Ox Road a little farther southwest, to Tarvasjoki (a former municipality, part of Lieto since 2015). Since Tarvasjoki is now part of Lieto, we can tick that municipality off as visited too. We will have to visit the centre of Lieto another time; for now it was time to leave the Ox Road and turn north.

![Tarvasjoki church](36-tarvasjoki_kyrka.jpg "Tarvasjoki church.")

Our first stop was the church village of Aura, a little south of the centre and right next to the border with present-day Lieto. Then we followed the Aura River upstream towards the municipal centre. The roads continued to be just as pleasant as before, and the rain let up a little between showers.

![Aura church](37-aura_kyrka.jpg "Aura church.")

Aura is not a large municipality. Just north of the centre, we entered the municipality of Pöytyä. We continued along the narrow, winding road beside the Aura River instead of taking the parallel route 41. Pöytyä’s municipal centre is the village of Riihikoski, while the Pöytyä church village lies five kilometres farther north along the river. Above the church door hangs a sign in Swedish: “Gud till ära under konung Gustaf IV Adolfs andra regeringsår 1793 är denna kyrka byggd” (“Built to the glory of God in the second regnal year of King Gustav IV Adolf, 1793”), even though the municipality is now monolingually Finnish.

![Pöytyä church](38-poytis_kyrka.jpg "Pöytyä church.")

We continued along the course of the Aura River. By the time we reached the centre of Oripää, the river was little more than a large ditch. The source of the Aura River is actually only a short distance north of the centre. Oripää is not a large place. The church seemed almost isolated, as it was on the other side of the main road.

We have ridden through the municipality of Oripää once before, but then only along road 213 through the village of Juvankoski, a curious exclave of Oripää squeezed between Säkylä and Loimaa.

![Oripää church](39-oripaa_kyrka.jpg "Oripää church.")

After that, we had to take the main road for a short stretch. A little later I turned off towards the village of Virttaa. The village used to be called Virtsajoki, roughly “piss ditch”, so although the current name is rather ugly, it is probably an improvement. The village itself is really cosy. The road through the middle of the village has been turned into a single-lane street with cycle and footpaths on either side. The idea is to make room for all road users. The same arrangement could also be found in Luopioinen.

Virttaa used to belong to the municipality of Alastaro, which has been part of the town of Loimaa since 2009. The village is also home to the well-known Alastaro Circuit. Although it was close by, we rode past this time. [The last time we rode through Alastaro](../2024-06-09-helsingfors/#day-2-heading-back-home), the rain stopped just there, but today the scattered showers continued farther north.

![Virttaa church](40-virttaa_kyrka.jpg "Virttaa church.")

To avoid riding on the main road the whole time, we soon turned off again, this time east towards Vampula (a former municipality, part of Huittinen since 2009). We followed the Loimijoki river for a while before it was time to return to the main road and head north again.

![Vampula church](41-vambula_kyrka.jpg "Vampula church.")

Now it was 30 km, first along road 41 up to Huittinen and then along road 12 farther north. Riding on country roads on a rainy day is miserable. The traffic is dense enough that there are always cars in front of you, throwing up a cloud of dirty water droplets that stick to your visor and get everywhere on the motorcycle and your clothes. If you try to ride more slowly, someone soon overtakes you, and then you are back in the spray again. Oncoming traffic also adds to the flying dirty water. That is why I avoid busy roads on rainy days like this whenever possible. The weather was a little better now; the sun even shone for a while now and then, but then another shower came along. Roads dry very slowly in autumn.

It was not until a little north of Huittinen that I could turn off the main road towards Kiikka. The municipality of Kiikka merged with the municipality of Keikyä in 1981, forming the municipality of Äetsä. Äetsä in turn merged with Vammala and Mouhijärvi to form the town of Sastamala in 2009.

In Kiikka, the Sale shop is directly across the road from the church. I bought some water and a sandwich, stowed my things and continued north.

![Kiikka church](42-kiikka_kyrka.jpg "Kiikka church.")

The road north was no longer so busy, so flying dirty water was no longer much of a problem. I did take a little detour through the village of Kiikoinen. Kiikoinen became part of the town of Sastamala in 2013. Like Äetsä, Kiikoinen used to belong to Satakunta, whereas Vammala (and later Sastamala) belongs to Pirkanmaa, so these municipalities changed regions as part of the municipal merger.

![Kiikoinen church](43-kikois_kyrka.jpg "Kiikoinen church.")

In Lavia (a former municipality, part of the city of Pori since 2015) I filled the tank. The last shower of rain came around here too. The farther north I rode, the more the sky cleared. At the same time, however, it got colder and the roads were still wet, so I kept my rain gear on.

![Lavia church](44-lavia_kyrka.jpg "Lavia church.")

A short ride through the centre of Kankaanpää, via the church. Then on through Honkajoki (a former municipality, part of Kankaanpää since 2021).

![Kankaanpää church](45-kankaanpaa_kyrka.jpg "Kankaanpää church.")

![Honkajoki church](46-honkajoki_kyrka.jpg "Honkajoki church.")

Halfway between Honkajoki and Isojoki, I had had enough of the rain gear. I turned onto a little forest road, took off my waterproofs and had a short coffee break. The weather had indeed become much colder. Even sitting in the sun in my motorcycle jacket with its winter lining, I no longer felt warm. Perhaps autumn had just arrived with the northerly wind. Instead of the rain gear, I put on my fleece and set the heated grips to winter mode. It was a little chilly, but manageable. I could also have put the winter lining in my riding trousers, but at the time I did not think it was worth the effort.

![Rain gear off](47-regnklader_av.jpg "Rain gear off.")

In Isojoki I got off the bike for one last stretch of my legs. Or, above all, to photograph what is described as “the world’s largest statue of a potato peeler”. I can believe it, but I find it a little hard to imagine that there is much competition.

![Isojoki church](48-stora_kyrka.jpg "Isojoki church.")

![Potato peeler](49-potatisskalare.jpg "Potato peeler.")

The last leg went via Karijoki and Myrkky and then onto national road 8 by the “Tomato” in Närpiö.

![Karijoki church](50-botom_kyrka.jpg "Karijoki church.")

We got home at 5 pm. Once again, I have to be grateful to have made it home without mishap, and a little wiser for the experience.


## Route

Two weeks ago, I wrote that the [ride to Turku](../2026-09-19-abo/) was the last long ride of the season. I also wrote that last year’s 11,103 kilometres in one season was a [record unlikely to be beaten](../2026-09-20-30k-tillsammans/#season-2025). How wrong you can be.

The plan was for this trip to be fairly short, but with October’s weather being so warm, it somehow grew longer. On the first day we rode (almost) straight to Helvetinjärvi National Park, 220 km. On the second day we continued south and covered 290 km. The third day turned into a decent 440 km stage, making a total of 950 km in three days. That surely counts as a long trip! We also passed last season’s total mileage. This year we have ridden 11,106 km, three kilometres more than last year. :smile: So far!

Little Blue’s odometer now reads 99,918 km. A CB 500’s odometer has only five digits and one decimal place, which means it will roll over to 00000.0 km after another 82 km. Unless the weather suddenly changes, that should happen this autumn. Will I have a brand-new bike then? Or will the bike automatically fall apart, just like in a Donald Duck story? Or will it be a repeat of Y2K – absolutely nothing changes?

![Route](rutt.webp "Route.")

My long-term goal is to ride my motorcycle, Little Blue, through every municipality and town in Finland. On this trip we visited six new municipalities. We have now visited 256 of Finland’s 308 municipalities.

New municipalities on this trip:

* Karkkila (Uusimaa)
* Koski Tl (Southwest Finland)
* Marttila (Southwest Finland)
* Lieto (Southwest Finland)
* Aura (Southwest Finland)
* Pöytyä (Southwest Finland)

We also rode through the centres of two places that we had previously only sped past on the outskirts: Loppi in Tavastia Proper and Oripää in Southwest Finland.
